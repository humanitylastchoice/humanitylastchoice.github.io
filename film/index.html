The idea

One choice, followed from the person to the model. A request is written by hand. Candidates arrive as cards on a field where every dot is a candidate. One card rewrites itself before our eyes, for the model instead of for the reader. The cursor, the mark’s own, crosses the field, hesitates, and lands on the rewritten card. Then the proof: on the dark ground, four systems, clean and shaped, and the gap. Then the tagline, and the name.

No narration. Music and type carry it, as the best product films do. Everything on screen is the brand: paper, ink, one madder accent, Newsreader and Plex, the dot lattice, the cursor, a found object. Nothing is decorated; the craft is in the timing.

Rhythm

72 beats per minute, 4/4: a beat is 0.833 s, a bar 3.333 s. Cuts land on bars; words land on beats; the cursor lands on a rest. Seventy-two seconds is 21.6 bars: the hero cut. Two cutdowns are built from the same shots: 30 s (the paper) and 15 s (the hook).

Motion rules

  • Easing is expo-out for arrivals and quint-in-out for camera moves; nothing is linear except the typewriter.
  • Type arrives by word: opacity, an 18-px rise and a 6-px blur that clears, 70 ms apart, last word on the beat.
  • The camera is a slow push or drift in every shot, never a zoom on a cut; the lattice moves at half the camera’s speed for depth.
  • One cursor, one landing. It slows before it lands; the ring appears after the click, not with it.
  • Cuts on bar lines; crossfades only inside a scene. Transitions are never wipes or slides.
  • Grain at 7 % over everything, as on the site. No gradients, no shadows, no 3D.

Sound

The score is synthesised by design/build_score.py from the film’s event list, so it stays in sync when a shot is re-timed: a slow pad in D minor moving to F major at the proof, a felt-piano motif on the beats, a sub pulse under the scoreboard, foley for the paper, the pen, the click and the landing. It is written to be replaceable: a licensed track (Musicbed, Artlist, Epidemic) at 72 BPM drops in with the same cut points; the foley stays. A voice-over is not planned; the on-screen text is the script.
